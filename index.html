Transparent Flood Decision Support

FloodGuard AI

A Malaysia-focused flood decision-support prototype that combines official monitoring, local sensing, CCTV verification, short-horizon forecasting and a human responder approval workflow before public alerts are released.

V4 rule: FloodGuard never sends a public warning directly from AI output. A possible flood must be reviewed by responders, team/resources must be arranged, response readiness confirmed, and only then can an authorised public Telegram alert be released. Live retrieval retains labelled official snapshot fallback behaviour.

Future Water-Level Forecast

What could this river station read 15–60 minutes from now?

The model predicts one thing only: the future JPS water-level reading for the selected station. FloodGuard then translates that number into an expected stage using the station's own official thresholds.

Simple flow: historical water levels → calculate recent rate of rise → train model → predict future water level → compare with official JPS thresholds. Rainfall is used only when the historical dataset actually contains usable rainfall values.

Rainfall Context

How much rain is falling, and why does FloodGuard care?

Rainfall is supporting context, not the official flood stage. It helps explain whether extra water is entering the catchment and may later contribute to a rising river.

FloodGuard use: show recent 1-hour rainfall → classify intensity using JPS bands → review recent rainfall history → combine rainfall with river-level history when a historical ML dataset contains rainfall. The live rainfall page is kept separate from the river station unless a defensible spatial/time match is available.

Official

Stage

Current water level is compared with that station's JPS Alert, Warning and Danger thresholds.

Derived

Rate of rise

(WL₂ − WL₁) / Δt. Only calculated from real distinct timestamps or explicit user inputs.

ML

OLS baseline

Chronological 80/20 train/test split. After validation, the model is refitted on all usable historical pairs and predicts a new water level beyond the latest timestamp. Metrics: MAE, RMSE, R² and persistence-baseline RMSE.

Validation rule

  • No random split: future readings are never used to train earlier predictions.
  • Benchmark required: regression is compared with “future level = current level”.
  • No fake confidence: model quality is shown through held-out errors.
  • No synthetic training rows: uploaded or bundled official historical observations only.

What V3 intentionally does not claim

  • Not an official warning service.
  • No “safe route” claim without verified road-closure / elevation data.
  • No resource-allocation claim without verified shelters, assets and response constraints.
  • No stream-flow dashboard while the current general official stream-flow page returns no usable rows.

Legend & Units

What the abbreviations and numbers mean

This legend is intentionally plain-language so users can interpret FloodGuard without engineering or hydrology background.

Hydrology & JPS

  • JPS — Jabatan Pengairan dan Saliran Malaysia (Department of Irrigation and Drainage, DID).
  • Sg. — Sungai / River.
  • Emp. — Empangan / Dam.
  • WL — Water Level parameter / station-ID suffix used by JPS.
  • RF — Rainfall parameter / station-ID suffix used by JPS.
  • F1 / F2 — suffixes present in JPS station names. FloodGuard preserves them but does not assign a meaning because the public pages reviewed do not define the suffix clearly.

Units

  • m — metres. For JPS water level, this is a station-referenced gauge/stage value; do not interpret it as river depth.
  • mm — millimetres of rainfall. Example: 0.0 mm in 1 hour means the gauge recorded no rain in that one-hour period.
  • m/h — metres per hour; used for rate of rise.
  • +15 / +30 / +60 min — how far into the future the AI model is asked to predict water level.

Flood stages

  • Normal — below Alert threshold.
  • Alert / Waspada — river level has risen significantly above Normal.
  • Warning / Amaran — river level is approaching flood level; prepare for evacuation if needed.
  • Danger / Bahaya — river level has reached a level associated with considerable flooding; evacuation may be required.

AI / validation

  • AI — Artificial Intelligence.
  • ML — Machine Learning.
  • OLS — Ordinary Least Squares linear regression; the current baseline prediction model.
  • MAE — Mean Absolute Error; average absolute prediction error in metres.
  • RMSE — Root Mean Square Error; prediction error metric that penalises larger misses more strongly.
  • R² — coefficient of determination; indicates how much variation the model explains on held-out data.
  • CSV — comma-separated values file used to import/export historical data and forecast reports.

Data labels

  • Official — retrieved from an official JPS source.
  • Snapshot — dated official data bundled for demonstration; not live.
  • Derived — calculated by FloodGuard from official/input data, e.g. distance to Danger or rate of rise.
  • ML — output from the trained machine-learning baseline.
